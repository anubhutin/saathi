# Content guidelines

## Voice

Warm, plain, active voice. Written for Indian women and families. Sentence case. No emoji, no jargon, no diagnosis. Buttons say what happens.

## Pathway structure

Each age pathway follows the same shape: who this is for, what is common at this age, what helps at home, when to talk to someone, who to talk to (named Indian services), and what is urgent. Wording comes from the team's problem matrix in `tools/content_pathways.py`.

## Resource metadata

Every resource carries: id, title, age groups, category, languages available, reading time, author, reviewer (name and role), review date, next review date, and a safety note where needed.

## Review workflow

1. Draft by a writer with relevant experience.
2. Review by a qualified professional (psychologist, psychiatrist, social worker or paediatrician as fits).
3. Translation by a native speaker who knows the subject, then a second native-speaker check.
4. Publish with the review date shown.
5. Re-review at least every 12 months, and sooner if a law, service or helpline changes. A lapsed review is flagged (see open decisions in `docs/safeguarding.md`).

## Translation

No machine-only translation for sensitive topics (self-harm, abuse, consent, violence, children's safety). Machine drafts may help a translator, never replace one. Maithili and Bhojpuri need translators from the communities that use them.

## Safe messaging

- Never describe methods of suicide or self-harm.
- Do not present suicide as a solution or as inevitable.
- Always give a route to help (Tele-MANAS 14416, 112 in danger).
- Do not blame survivors. Avoid "should" about leaving a home.
- Use everyday Indian examples with respect for family, faith and community. Include a gentle health check alongside traditional or faith-based support.
