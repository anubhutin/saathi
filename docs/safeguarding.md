# Safeguarding

## Principles

- Safety before data: if someone is in danger, help comes first and paperwork second.
- People decide. Software never sets priority, diagnoses or escalates by itself.
- Say plainly what we can and cannot keep private. Never promise absolute confidentiality.
- Children's welfare comes first, and the law on reporting applies.
- Respect culture and faith while keeping people safe.

The team's problem matrix, with the escalation route for each age group, is in `tools/content_pathways.py` (`ESCALATION`). Treat that file as the single source and update it, not copies of it.

## Laws that shape our practice (plain-words summary, not legal advice)

- POCSO Act 2012: anyone who knows or suspects that a child has been sexually abused must report it to the police or the Special Juvenile Police Unit. Failing to report is an offence. Our staff follow the organisation's reporting process on the same day.
- Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005: women facing violence at home can seek help from a Protection Officer, a One Stop Centre (Sakhi) and the courts. We explain these routes and do not tell a woman what to do.
- Mental Healthcare Act 2017: people have a right to access mental health care. Section 115 presumes that a person who attempts suicide is under severe stress and should get care, not punishment.
- Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023: personal data needs a clear purpose and consent. For children, verifiable parental consent is needed. Take advice on exceptions.
- Information Technology Rules: platforms must handle unlawful content and grievances properly. We publish a grievance contact before launch.

## Open decisions

1. Under-18 referral flow: how we get verifiable parental or guardian consent without putting a child at risk when the concern is about the family, and when a mandatory report overrides consent.
2. Who owns content review, how often, and what happens when a review date lapses (suggested: the page shows "under review" and a named person is alerted).
3. Who the safeguarding lead is, and who covers when they are away.
4. How reports are made to the Child Welfare Committee, the District Child Protection Unit or the police in each state where we work.
5. Which languages need a human interpreter during safeguarding conversations.
