Ageing and caregiving

Feeling lonely in later life

Ways to stay connected after retirement, bereavement or when children live far away.

Article 4 minutes Ageing and caregiving English

Who it is for: Older people and their families.

Format: Text, about 4 minutes to read

Loneliness is common in later life. Children move to other cities or countries, friends pass away, retirement changes your days, and a spouse's loss leaves a silence. Feeling lonely is human. It is not a weakness.

Ideas that older people have found helpful

  • A regular daily routine: morning walk, prayers, tea at a set time.
  • A fixed time to speak with children or grandchildren by phone or video. WhatsApp voice notes are easy.
  • Local groups: a bhajan mandali, a temple, mosque, church or gurdwara group, a park laughter club, a senior citizens' club.
  • Helping others: teaching a skill, reading to children or sharing a recipe.
  • A hobby: gardening, radio, newspaper, knitting, storytelling.

If you have lost someone

Grief takes its own time. Cry if you need to. Talk about the person. If sadness stays heavy, or you stop eating or sleeping, a doctor can help. It is not weakness to ask.

For families

Call regularly, and ask about their day, not only about health. Include them in decisions and festivals. Small, steady contact matters more than big gestures.

Elderline 14567 is a free helpline for senior citizens. If you feel hopeless or want to end your life, call Tele-MANAS on 14416 at any hour.

This is general information. It is not a substitute for care from a doctor or counsellor. If someone is in immediate danger, call 112.
