Emotional wellbeing

When days feel difficult

Small, kind steps for days when everything feels heavy, and when to reach out.

Guide 4 minutes Emotional wellbeing English

Who it is for: Teenagers and adults who feel low, tired or overwhelmed.

Format: Text, about 4 minutes to read

Everyone has hard days. Sometimes it comes after a loss, a fight at home, a failed exam, or for no reason at all. Feeling low does not mean you are weak or 'pagal'. It means you are a person carrying something.

Small things that can help today

  • Eat something and drink water. A tired, hungry body makes everything look darker.
  • Step outside, even to the balcony or the lane, for five minutes of daylight.
  • Do one small task, like washing your face or tidying one shelf. Let that be enough.
  • Message or sit with one person who is kind to you. You do not have to explain everything.
  • If it is your faith practice, prayer or quiet time can be a comfort. It sits well alongside other support.

When to reach out for more

Please speak to a doctor, counsellor or helpline if the low mood lasts more than two weeks, if sleep or appetite has changed a lot, if you cannot do daily things, or if you feel hopeless. Getting care is your right under the Mental Healthcare Act 2017, and it is not a sign of failure. It is fine to see a doctor at a PHC or district hospital, and a traditional or faith healer too, if that matters to your family.

If you have thoughts of ending your life, you are not alone and help is available. Tele-MANAS (14416) is free and open all day and night. If you are in immediate danger, call 112 or tell a person near you right now.

This is general information. It is not a substitute for care from a doctor or counsellor. If someone is in immediate danger, call 112.
