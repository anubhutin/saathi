Stress and anxiety

Breathing for hard moments

A slow breathing exercise for when worry or anger feels too big.

Activity 2 minutes Stress and anxiety English

Who it is for: Anyone aged about 8 and above, in a quiet corner, a bathroom or a bus seat.

Format: Text, about 2 minutes to read

Press start when you are ready.

Breathe in as the circle grows (4 seconds), pause (2), then breathe out slowly as it shrinks (6). Stop whenever you like.

When we are scared or upset, our breathing gets quick and shallow, and that makes the feeling louder. Slow breathing, with a longer breath out than in, tells the body it is safe to settle. It does not fix the problem. It gives you a little room to think.

Try it for one minute

  1. Sit or stand comfortably. Let your shoulders drop. You can close your eyes or look at one spot.
  2. Breathe in through your nose, slowly, counting to 4.
  3. Pause for a moment.
  4. Breathe out gently through your mouth, counting to 6, as if cooling a cup of chai.
  5. Repeat for about six rounds. Notice if your shoulders feel a little softer.

If counting makes you dizzy or more anxious, stop and breathe the way you normally do. Look around the room and name three things you can see. Nothing here has to be done perfectly.

You can do this before an exam paper, after an argument, in a crowded local train, or at night when your mind will not stop. Many people find it works better the more often they practise on easy days.

If hard moments come very often, or you feel unsafe, please talk to someone you trust. Tele-MANAS (14416) offers free support at any hour.

This is general information. It is not a substitute for care from a doctor or counsellor. If someone is in immediate danger, call 112.
