Ageing and caregiving

Looking after yourself while you care

Rest, support and permission to need help, for people who care for others every day.

Guide 4 minutes Ageing and caregiving English

Who it is for: Mothers, daughters-in-law, grandparents and anyone who cares for a child, a sick person or an elder.

Format: Text, about 4 minutes to read

Caring for others can become a life with no breaks. Mothers get up first and sleep last. Grandmothers care for grandchildren while managing their own aches. Nobody notices when you are running on empty.

Signs you may need support

  • You are tired in a way that rest does not fix.
  • You feel irritable, tearful or numb.
  • You have stopped doing things you used to enjoy.
  • You have aches, headaches or poor sleep.
  • You feel guilty when you take any time for yourself.

Small ways to refill

  • Take ten minutes in the day that are yours: tea, a short prayer, music, a stretch.
  • Ask for something specific. 'Can you take Mohan to school on Tuesday?' is easier to answer than a general plea.
  • Eat and drink at proper times, not after everyone else.
  • Talk to another woman who understands: a neighbour, a self-help group member, a friend.
  • Visit the doctor for your own health too.

Rest is not selfish. A caregiver who is supported gives better care, and a child, a patient or an elder is calmer when you are.

If you feel you cannot go on, or have thoughts of harming yourself or the person you care for, call Tele-MANAS on 14416 or see a doctor.

This is general information. It is not a substitute for care from a doctor or counsellor. If someone is in immediate danger, call 112.
